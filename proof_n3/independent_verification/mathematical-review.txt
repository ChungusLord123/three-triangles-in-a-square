Independent local mathematical review

Problem: three congruent unit equilateral triangles inside a square, with
disjoint interiors. Boundary contact is allowed. Claimed minimum square side:
sqrt(3)/2 + sqrt(6)/4. This review was written by the same assistant that
conducted the search, using separate derivations and implementations. It is
not external peer review and is not a formal proof-assistant derivation.

1. Precisely define the predicates being certified

For each encoded profile, P(x) means a packing with that FULL active wall
profile and those actual pair-contact types. A zero pair code denotes no
contact when assigning a full profile. Vertex-edge means contact in the
relative interior of the edge; edge-edge means positive-length overlap.
Unrecorded walls and pairs have strict slack in a full profile.

This qualification is essential. The same numeric row can contain geometric
boundary points whose actual full profile has extra contacts. Such points
belong to a different P. In particular, outside-the-owner-edge inequalities
for a proper vertex-edge contact need not hold for an arbitrary endpoint
vertex-vertex contact. Endpoint points are assigned vertex-vertex profiles.
The checked contractions preserve every point of P in the parent box; they
are not claims about every possible interpretation of a requirement prefix.

2. Every possible global minimum has an encoded profile

The independently checked construction gives a feasible upper bound S0.
Any square can be translated and rotated into the centred axis-aligned frame
without changing its side or the admissible triangle shapes. Centroids are
convex averages of contained vertices and hence lie inside that square.
Rotations form a compact circle. Containment and disjoint interiors are closed
conditions, expressible through finitely many polygon supporting axes.
Restricting side lengths to [0,S0] therefore gives an attained global minimum.
Area gives 16*S^4 >= 27 and thus S>1. The construction also gives S0<2.

Each unit triangle has diameter one, so it cannot touch opposite walls.
A touched wall supports a vertex or an edge. A square corner, if contained in
a triangle, must be a triangle vertex, since it is an extreme point. Two
60-degree triangle interior sectors cannot fit disjointly in its 90-degree
quadrant. These facts justify the wall-mask structural and corner exclusions.

The intersection of two convex triangles with disjoint interiors is empty,
a single boundary point, or a boundary segment. A point is vertex-vertex or
proper vertex-edge: two intersecting relative edge interiors either create
interior overlap or share a positive segment. A positive segment is edge-edge.
The 37 pair modes therefore cover all actual contacts. Cyclic vertex labels,
triangle permutations and square D4 transformations preserve the problem;
reflections reverse vertex order before returning to CCW labels.

Vertex support cones have half-angle 60 degrees, proper VE imposes the
corresponding outside-halfplane cone, VV interior sectors require centre-ray
separation at least 60 degrees, and positive EE requires opposite normals.
All cone endpoints and difference equalities have integer offsets in units
of 30 degrees. Flooring unwrapped real angular variables preserves their
integer difference bounds/equalities and circular branches. The separate
15-degree relation join verified every finite angular membership, every
geometric profile reduction and every component-criticality membership.
This does not restrict actual packing orientations to a finite grid.

3. Necessary minimum-profile reductions

Contact components can be translated rigidly. If none spans opposite walls,
shrink the square and translate each component at velocity +/-1/2 along its
touched coordinate directions. Contacts within a component are preserved.
Untouched wall clearances and distances between components are strictly
positive in P, so sufficiently small translations preserve feasibility.
Such a full profile cannot contain a global minimum.

Two unit bases cannot share a wall when S<2: their base intervals overlap in
positive length, and both triangles have interior on the same side. Proper
VE or positive EE cannot use a container-wall edge for the same local reason.

For a component whose sole determining span has unique supporting vertices,
the span after a small rigid rotation is A*cos(theta)+B*sin(theta)>0. Its second
derivative is its negative. A nonzero first derivative permits descent; zero
first derivative is a strict local maximum. The other dimension has slack.
Thus a minimum requires some component with both dimensions binding or
multiple labelled supports on a determining wall. Coincident labels only
overcount cases and hence do not exclude a legitimate minimum.

For multiple supports, the positive-angle width derivative is
max(y_left)-min(y_right); the negative-angle derivative is
min(y_left)-max(y_right). Vertical derivatives use the analogous top/bottom
x coordinates. A zero determining derivative still gives second-order
decrease through the negative curvature. Both binding dimensions must share
a decreasing angle sign. Other vertices remain inactive for a sufficiently
small angle. Every component can then be shifted continuously to fit the
slightly smaller centred square. Strict distances between components persist.
The new verifier separately checked each recorded rotation removal.
Rigidly moving an entire component does not assume that it is internally
rigid: flexible and singular components admit that motion too.

4. Continuous root coverage and geometry semantics

Every proper unit triangle has the canonical CCW body vertices
(1/2,-h/3),(0,2h/3),(-1/2,-h/3), h=sqrt(3)/2, up to a rotation and translation.
Their sum is zero, their edges are unit, their area is h/2, and their outward
normal/support tables are checked independently in Q(sqrt(2),sqrt(3)).

Two rational charts cover the whole rotation circle. For c>=0 on the unit
circle, t=s/(1+c) lies in [-1,1] and recovers c=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2),
s=2t/(1+t^2). For c<0, apply this to (-c,-s) and negate the resulting matrix.
Seams appear in both charts. All eight piece-chart combinations are supplied.
The independently generated initial centroid, rotation and side bounds match
all implicit roots. The initial area lower bound satisfies the exact
128-bit inequality 16*L^4 <= 27*Q^4. All shell starts overlap the previous
completed rational target. Every coordinate in the precision lift scales
exactly; all old terminal regions are reconsidered. Target clipping discards
only regions with side lower bound above the target ceiling.

Halfspace conditions use unit edge normals. Contact heights from opposite
vertices are h for VE and 2h for EE. A triangle's minimum projection width is
h: its width is the maximum absolute projection of its three unit edges, and
the six directed edge rays are spaced 60 degrees apart. One is within 30
degrees of any projection direction. The six edge-normal separating axes
are complete for triangle pairs by the convex polygon separating-axis
property; Minkowski-difference edges come from edges of the two triangles.

The new interval implementation uses guarded signed integer products, outward
bit-shift floor/ceiling, signed quotient bounds, and a full 64x64-to-128-bit
product for rational rotation endpoints. Its radical search uses unsigned
squares bounded by 9*Q^2<2^64. Unknown arithmetic disables certification.
The rotation derivative identities establish monotone endpoint bounds.
Vector-radius clipping, containment, support equalities, unit distances and
proper-contact membership conditions are necessary for P. Equality and
halfspace contractors intersect with outward enclosures of necessary
coordinate expressions. Solving a halfspace with a negative normal component
uses the quotient's upper endpoint; positive uses its lower endpoint.

For every successful producer contraction, the independent enclosure either
proves P empty or is contained inside the recorded contracted rectangle.
Every producer physical rejection is likewise checked separately. The
existing exact parent queues, splits and journal commitments establish the
tree's bookkeeping; no unexplored region is silently counted as excluded.

5. Independently checked strict motions

Set side derivative to -1. Active wall violation derivatives must be strictly
negative. For an owner supporting normal n, gap derivative is
n dot (v_other-v_owner) + omega_owner*Jn dot (centre_delta+local_other)
- omega_other*Jn dot local_other.
This follows by differentiating the rotating support plane and using the
skew symmetry of J. It is independent of the floating witness proposer.

For VV, the owner endpoint tangent coefficient is +/-1/2 on an incident edge,
or zero on its radial support plane. Proper VE gives owner tangent coefficient
in [-1/2,1/2]. Positive EE gives an owner coefficient in [-3/2,3/2] and other
endpoint coefficients exactly -1/2,+1/2. These bounds follow from the unit
segment projections and positive segment overlap. Noncontact vertices on
the chosen axis have strictly positive initial gaps. All other absent
contacts/walls have strict slack under P. Strict derivative inequalities
therefore give a smaller feasible square for each actual point of P after
a sufficiently small positive time. No uniform step over the closed numeric
box is asserted or required. The new verifier checks supplied dyadic
velocities directly, and independently checks every recorded removal.

6. Independent exact boundary-family proof and construction

After square symmetry put the corner vertex at A=(0,0), and opposite bases
on the right and top walls. Their apices are P=(u,z1), T=(z2,u), u=S-h,
zi in [1/2,S-1/2]. The independent window check puts every shell side strictly
between (sqrt(3)+1)/2 and sqrt(3). It verifies h-u>0 and
sqrt(3)*u-(S-1/2)>0, so the apices lie strictly in the common 30-to-60-degree
cone of every possible corner triangle. Nonoverlap therefore forces
A1=nx*u+ny*z1-h>=0 and A2=nx*z2+ny*u-h>=0, where the corner opposite-edge
unit normal has nx,ny>0 and nx^2+ny^2=1.

The right/top wall axes cannot separate the opposite contained unit bases.
Their two non-facing sloped axes have a strictly negative gap at an opposite
base endpoint: u<h<1, zi+1/2-u>=1-u>0, and S-zi>=1/2. Only the two facing
sloped edges remain. Their apex inequalities are respectively
CL=(1+sqrt(3))*u-z1-sqrt(3)*z2>=0 or
CR=(1+sqrt(3))*u-sqrt(3)*z1-z2>=0.

Eliminating z1,z2 gives the independently checked positive dual identities
nx*A1+sqrt(3)*ny*A2+nx*ny*CL=(nx+sqrt(3)*ny)*(u*(nx+ny)-h), and
sqrt(3)*nx*A1+ny*A2+nx*ny*CR=(sqrt(3)*nx+ny)*(u*(nx+ny)-h).
Hence u*(nx+ny)>=h. Unit-normal Cauchy gives nx+ny<=sqrt(2), so
S>=h+h/sqrt(2)=h+q, q=sqrt(6)/4. This proof treats independent sliding apices
and arbitrary corner orientation. It imposes neither a rank condition nor
an apex-equality assumption. Every analytic wall classification matches.

The earlier specialised shared-apex rule is valid even below this window:
the coded contacts force P=(u,u) and the corner-edge equation
u*(nx+ny)=h. Since S>1>h, u>0, and Cauchy again gives S>=h+q.

With a=sqrt(2)/4, the exact upper construction is
corner [(0,0),(q+a,q-a),(q-a,q+a)],
right [(q,q),(h+q,q-1/2),(h+q,q+1/2)],
top [(q,q),(q+1/2,h+q),(q-1/2,h+q)].
All unit distances and positive containment margins are independently checked
in the four-element field basis. The plane x+y=2q separates the corner from
the other triangles. The plane through (q,q) with normal (-1/2,h) separates
the right and top triangles, with vertex gaps (0,-h,0) and (0,1/2,1).
This achieves the lower-bound value without interior overlaps.

7. Conclusion and remaining trust

An actual better packing would imply an attained global minimum below S0.
Its full proper profile and continuous parameters are represented by the
audited root domains. The independently checked inference tree would either
exclude it as infeasible/non-minimum or put it in an analytic family with
S>=S0. Each disposition contradicts a smaller global minimum. The exact
construction attains S0. This is the computer-assisted certificate's global
minimum claim.

The implementation still trusts GPU/compiler integer semantics, the file/hash
and evidence-adapter bookkeeping, and the written mathematical lemmas above.
These have separate implementation checks and a local source review, not a
formal proof assistant or external mathematical peer review. No external
reviewer was contacted and no claim of publication or novelty is made.
