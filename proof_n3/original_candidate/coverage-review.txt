Three unit equilateral triangles in a square: coverage argument

Claimed minimum: s0 = sqrt(3)/2 + sqrt(6)/4.
This is the mathematical argument accompanying the replayable computation.
It is a proof candidate, not an externally reviewed or proof-assistant-checked
theorem. The saved numerical receipts deliberately retain their original
scope; they do not independently verify every implication in this argument.

1. Existence of a minimum and a complete finite contact description

The explicit construction in geometric_semantics_certificate.json has three
unit triangles in [0,s0]^2 with disjoint interiors. Restricting the container
side to at most s0 gives bounded centroid coordinates and compact rotation
circles. Containment and disjoint interiors are closed conditions: for two
convex polygons, disjoint interiors mean that some edge-normal separating
axis has nonnegative minimum gap. There are finitely many such axes.
Therefore a minimum is attained. Area gives 16*s^4 >= 27, hence s>1.

A triangle of diameter one cannot touch opposite walls when s>1. Its touched
walls are consequently at most two adjacent walls. A wall supports either a
vertex or a full edge. Each vertex-wall incidence is one of 12 bits, so the
4096 wall masks cover every possible wall contact set. Vertex support cones
have endpoints at multiples of 30 degrees. Two positive-area triangles cannot
share a square corner: their two 60-degree interior sectors cannot be disjoint
inside a 90-degree corner.

For a triangle pair with disjoint interiors, the boundary intersection is
empty, a point, or a segment. Point contact is vertex-vertex or a vertex in the
relative interior of an edge. An intersection of the relative interiors of
two nonparallel edges would give overlapping interiors. Segment contact is
positive-length edge-edge overlap, including coincident complete edges.
There are therefore exactly the 37 encoded pair possibilities. Endpoint and
zero-length degeneracies use vertex-vertex modes. No rigidity or rank test
discards a family. Cyclic labels, all six piece permutations and square D4
symmetry preserve coverage. Reflections reverse vertex order.

The angular tests are necessary, not sufficient. Their closed cone bounds
and angular differences have integer endpoints in units of 30 degrees.
Choose unwrapped circular branches of any real angular solution. Flooring
each unwrapped angle preserves integer difference bounds and equalities.
Thus failure of the finite angular test rules out that real solution. The
independent MPS relation-join checker verifies every one of the 16,918,102
catalogue decisions against the Metal triple-loop enumeration.

2. Necessary conditions on the FULL contact profile of an attained minimum

Contacts define connected components of triangles. Components can be moved
rigidly. If no component spans opposite walls, translate each component
inward while shrinking the square. Its recorded wall contacts are preserved;
untouched walls and other components have strictly positive gaps. Such a
profile cannot be a minimum.

A proper vertex-edge contact or edge-edge overlap cannot use an edge on the
container wall: both triangles would have interior on the same side there.
Two unit bases cannot share a wall of length s<2, since their base intervals
must overlap in positive length and cause interior overlap.

If a component spans only one opposite-wall pair and has just one labelled
support vertex on each determining wall, a small rigid rotation decreases
its span. Locally that span is A*cos(theta)+B*sin(theta), and its second
derivative is minus the positive span. A nonzero first derivative supplies
a decreasing direction; a zero first derivative is a strict local maximum.
The other dimension has slack. Thus some component of a minimum spans both
wall pairs, or has multiple labelled supports on a determining wall.

The stronger support-coordinate rotation test uses the one-sided derivatives
max(y_left)-min(y_right) and min(y_left)-max(y_right), with the analogous
vertical expressions. If a determining derivative is zero, all active span
differences with that derivative still have negative second derivative.
The saved interval tests remove a region only when a decreasing direction
is certified for every relevant component and binding dimension.

These are reductions of FULL active profiles, not infeasibility claims about
requirement prefixes. A boundary solution with extra contacts is represented
by its actual full profile elsewhere in the catalogue. This distinction is
also essential for all strict-descent certificates below.

5. Exact lower bound for the surviving analytic family

After square symmetry, suppose one triangle has vertex A=(0,0), and the other
two have complete unit bases on the right and top walls. Write u=s-h, and
their inward apices P=(u,z1), T=(z2,u), with zi in [1/2,s-1/2]. The shell lies
strictly within (sqrt(3)+1)/2 < s < sqrt(3). Consequently each apex lies
strictly in the common 30-to-60-degree cone of every triangle supported at A.
Nonoverlap with the corner triangle forces
A1=nx*u+ny*z1-h >= 0 and A2=nx*z2+ny*u-h >= 0,
where the opposite-edge unit normal satisfies nx,ny>0 and nx^2+ny^2=1.

For the right/top pair, the right and top wall axes cannot separate their
contained unit bases. The other two outward sloping axes each have a strictly
negative gap at one of the opposite triangle's wall-base endpoints, using
u<h<1 and the base-containment ranges. Thus a separating axis must be one of
the two facing sloping edges. Its apex inequality implies
C=(1+k)*u-z1-k*z2 >= 0 for k=sqrt(3) or 1/sqrt(3).

The independently checked polynomial identity is
nx*A1 + k*ny*A2 + nx*ny*C = (nx+k*ny)*(u*(nx+ny)-h).
All weights and the right prefactor are positive, so u*(nx+ny)>=h.
Since the unit normal has nx+ny<=sqrt(2), this gives s>=h+h/sqrt(2)=s0.
The independent polynomial audit also checks the equivalent sum-of-squares
identity. No pair-contact equality or restriction to a rigid configuration
is assumed. The entire family, including its equality case and sliding or
singular limits, is covered. The seed's wall-profile classification and all
remaining continuous roots are independently checked on MPS.

6. Exhaustive disposition and exact feasible upper bound

The numerical part excludes every minimum profile through s=1.4783977.
The exact-boundary shell begins at its conservative dyadic floor and ends
at a dyadic upper enclosure of s0. It therefore includes every remaining
possible smaller minimum. Its 8,257 analytically covered profiles have the
lower bound s0. All other profiles and all their descendants have either
physical infeasibility or a verified non-minimum certificate. The final
active and precision-limit frontiers are both empty.

Let q=sqrt(6)/4, a=sqrt(2)/4, S=h+q. The feasible construction is
corner: (0,0), (q+a,q-a), (q-a,q+a);
right: (q,q), (S,q-1/2), (S,q+1/2);
top: (q,q), (q+1/2,S), (q-1/2,S).
The independent exact coefficient checks establish all unit edges. Positive
squared margins establish h>1/2, q>1/2, h>a and q>a, so every vertex is in
the square. The plane x+y=2q separates the corner triangle from both others.
The plane with normal (-1/2,h) through (q,q) separates the right and top
triangles: its vertex gaps are (0,-h,0) and (0,1/2,1). Interiors are disjoint.
Thus this construction attains s0, matching the exhaustive lower-bound claim.
